# Launch & Hand Off — the runbook

**Open this when a prospect can't fund a monthly plan.** Every step, in order, from the
sentence you say on the call to the follow-up six months later.

**Bryson, 2026-08-19:** *"write a step by step how to do all of that so I dont have to
reask you or wing it when the time comes."*

> **This product is NOT advertised.** It is not on the website, not in the package
> recommender, not in any email. It exists so you have something honest to offer when a
> monthly plan is genuinely the wrong answer. If you publish it, people who could afford
> managed will pick the cheap thing before you've said a word.

---

## What it is, in one line

**$1,500 once. You build the whole thing, run it for 30 days, hand them the keys, and walk
away.** No monthly, no per-lead fee, no contract term.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price** | $1,500, paid once, before you start building |
| **They get** | Campaign + custom landing page + call tracking + conversion tracking, all in **their** account. Two optimization passes over 30 days. A written playbook and a training call. |
| **They don't get** | Any ongoing management. No reports, no optimization, no lead follow-up after day 30. |
| **The hook** | Move onto a monthly plan within **6 months** and their setup fee is **waived**. |
| **Hosting** | **They** host the landing page. You give them the file and a guide. |

## STEP 0 — Should you even offer it?

**Offer it when the honest answer is "a monthly plan won't work for you":**

- Their ad budget is **under $500/mo**. That's the hard floor for managed.
- They can't commit to three months.
- They've said no to the monthly **twice** and you believe them.

**Do NOT offer it when:**

- They can afford managed and are just negotiating. Managed makes them more money. Hold.
- They want hand-off pricing with managed service. Say no once, plainly.
- They don't answer their phone. Same rule as managed — the campaign will fail and it'll
  be your name on it.

**Never lead with it.** Recommend managed first, every time. This comes out only after
managed has been ruled out.

## STEP 1 — On the call

### Say this

> "Let me offer you something different, because I don't think the monthly plan is right
> for you and I'd rather say that than sell it to you anyway.
>
> At your budget there isn't enough data coming through for me to optimize anything
> meaningfully month to month. You'd be paying me to watch a campaign that doesn't have
> enough to learn from. So instead: I build the whole thing once, properly. The campaign,
> a landing page, call tracking, all of it in your account, in your name.
>
> I run it for the first 30 days and fix what needs fixing. Then I write you a plain
> English playbook, we do a call so you know exactly what to look at each week, and it's
> yours. **$1,500, one time. No monthly, no per-lead fee, nothing after that.**
>
> And if you grow into it and want me running it later, the setup fee comes off, because
> you already paid me to build it."

### Then say the hosting part before they ask

> "One thing so there are no surprises later. The landing page ends up on your hosting,
> not mine. I hand you the file and a step by step guide, it takes about three minutes and
> it's free. That's deliberate. It means nothing I own can switch off and take your page
> down with it."

### Answers to the three questions they'll ask

**"Why is this more than your setup fee on the monthly plan?"**
> "Because the setup fee on a monthly plan is deliberately cheap. I make it back over the
> months you stay. Here there are no months, so the build has to pay for itself. You're
> also getting more than a setup: the first 30 days of me fixing things, and the playbook
> and the call so you can actually run it."

**"Can I do this and then hire you monthly later?"**
> "That's exactly how it's meant to work, and if you do it within six months I'll waive
> the setup fee. You already paid me to build it, so charging you twice would be a bit
> rich."

**"What if I break something?"**
> "The playbook has a section called leave this alone, and it's the most important part of
> the document. It tells you exactly which settings will quietly cost you money if you
> touch them."

### Before you hang up

Get these written down. You need them for the build:

- [ ] Their **real business phone number** (not a tracking number — theirs)
- [ ] Their **monthly ad budget**
- [ ] What they sell, and the **one service** to advertise first
- [ ] Their **service area**
- [ ] Who **answers the phone** during the day
- [ ] Do they already have a Google Ads account, or do you need to help them make one

## STEP 2 — Set them up in the OS

1. Open the OS, press **+ Add Client**.
2. Fill in their name, contact name, **email**, phone, niche, service area, ad budget.
3. Package: choose **Launch & Hand Off**.
4. Save.

You'll now see a **Launch & Hand Off** card at the top of their Overview tab. That card is
your checklist for everything below.

---

## STEP 3 — Get paid, before you build

**Never start the build before the money lands.** This is a one-time sale — there's no
second invoice to catch up on.

1. Client page → **Contract** tab.
2. Press **Send Contract**. It sends the hand-off version, which is a different agreement
   from the managed one: no term, no renewal, no early-termination fee, and a clause
   saying your obligations end at handover.
3. Once they've signed, on the same tab press **Set Up Billing**.
4. It creates a **one-time** payment link — not a subscription. Send them the link.
5. When they pay, the OS stamps the date and **the clock starts by itself.**

**If they pay some other way** (bank transfer, cash, an invoice you sent by hand): open the
hand-off card and press **Start the hand-off clock**. Nothing downstream works without
that date.

---

## STEP 4 — Build it (days 1 to 7)

Exactly the same as a managed client. Nothing different here.

1. **Campaigns** tab → build the Google campaign. Leave it **paused**.
2. **Client View** tab → **Generate Landing Page**, then **Publish**.
3. Set up **conversion tracking** in *their* Google Ads account.
   - Google Ads → **Goals** → **Conversions** → **New conversion action** → **Website**
   - You'll get a **conversion ID** (looks like `AW-123456789`) and a **label**.
   - **Write both down. You need them in step 6 and there is no way to guess them.**
4. Call tracking: you *can* use a tracking number for the first 30 days. It gives you
   better data during the settle-in. **But it comes off the page at handover** — see the
   warning in step 6.
5. Set their daily budget, then **unpause**.

---

## STEP 5 — The settle-in (days 1 to 30)

This is the part they paid for that a freelancer wouldn't do. **Two optimization passes.**

**Around day 10:**
- Add negative keywords for anything irrelevant that showed up
- Pause any keyword with lots of clicks and zero leads
- Check the landing page actually converts on a phone

**Around day 25:**
- Second pass, same idea
- Make sure conversions are being counted in Google Ads
- Sanity-check cost per lead against what you told them on the call

The OS will nudge you as the 30 days run down. If you haven't sent the handover pack by
then, it turns **red** — because they paid for that pack.

---

## STEP 6 — Export the landing page

**Do this before the training call**, so you can walk them through it live.

> ### 🔴 Why you can't just send them the page
> The page you see at your `/lp/` link is built fresh on your server every time someone
> visits. It isn't a file. If you never do this step, their page dies the day you stop
> hosting it, and three things break silently long before that: the form posts to your
> server (leads vanish), the phone number is one you rent (goes dead), and Google stops
> counting conversions (their campaign quietly gets worse at finding customers).

1. Hand-off card → **Export their landing page**.
2. **Their real phone number** — the one they answer. **Not the tracking number.** The OS
   will refuse without it, on purpose.
3. **Conversion ID** and **label** from step 4. Leave blank only if you truly don't have
   them yet, and go get them — without a tag their campaign stops learning.
4. Their domain, if they want to use their own web address. Optional.
5. Press **Build the export**.
6. Save **both** files: `index.html` and `HOW-TO-PUT-THIS-ONLINE.txt`.
7. Put them in **one folder** and send them that folder.

---

## STEP 7 — Write and send the handover pack

1. Hand-off card → **Write the handover pack**. Takes a few seconds.
2. Press **Read it** and actually read it. It's written for their specific campaign, and
   you're the one whose name is on it.
3. Send it. Copy-as-text puts it straight into an email.
4. **Book the training call** while you're sending it.

---

## STEP 8 — The training call (about 30 minutes)

Walk them through it live. Screen share.

1. **Put their page online with them on the call.** Open
   [app.netlify.com/drop](https://app.netlify.com/drop), drag the folder, done in a minute.
2. **Turn on lead emails together.** Netlify → their site → **Forms** → *Settings and
   usage* → *Form notifications* → *Add notification* → *Email notification*.
   **Do not skip this.** Without it Netlify collects their leads and tells nobody.
3. **Send a test enquiry through the form** and wait for the email to land. Don't end the
   call until you've both seen it arrive.
4. **Repoint their ads.** Google Ads → Campaigns → their campaign → Ads → edit each ad →
   change **Final URL** to the new address. Every ad. Miss one and it sends people to a
   page that's about to disappear.
5. **Walk the playbook.** Spend most of your time on "leave this alone".
6. Tell them plainly: *"from here it's yours. I'm not watching it. Call me if X, Y or Z."*

---

## STEP 9 — Close it out

1. Hand-off card → **Mark handed over**.
2. **Remove your manager access** to their Google Ads account. They own it outright, and
   staying in it implies you're still watching.
3. **Release the call tracking number** if you used one. Client page → call tracking →
   release. Otherwise you keep paying Twilio for a number nobody's using.

The moment you mark it handed over, the OS stops treating them as a client: no reports, no
autopilot, no lead follow-up. That's correct. They were promised nothing further.

---

## STEP 10 — The six-month window

This is where the money is. They've now run it themselves for a while, which is the best
possible moment to ask how it's going.

The OS reminds you as the waiver gets close. **Call them.**

> "You've had it running a few months now. How's it going? Still getting the calls?
>
> Heads up, the setup credit I mentioned runs out at the end of [month]. If you ever want
> me taking it back over, doing it before then means no setup fee. After that it'd be full
> price."

**When they say yes:** change their package in the OS. **The setup fee zeroes itself** and
the alert tells you it did. Don't charge them a setup fee — they already paid for the
build.

**When the window closes:** the OS says so. Still worth a call, it just costs them a setup
fee now.

## Things that will bite you

| Mistake | What happens |
|---|---|
| Building before they pay | There's no second invoice. You've done a $1,500 build for free. |
| Exporting with the **tracking number** on it | Their page shows a dead number weeks later. Nobody connects the two events. The OS blocks this. |
| Skipping the **conversion tag** | Google can't see conversions, so it can't bid. The campaign degrades over weeks while looking fine. |
| Skipping **lead email notifications** | Netlify collects their leads and tells nobody. They think the ads failed. |
| Forgetting to **repoint the ads** | They pay for clicks that land on a page you're about to switch off. |
| Leaving your **manager access** in place | Implies you're still watching. You're not. |
| Not **releasing the tracking number** | You keep paying for it forever. |
| Doing "just one small favour" after handover | Teaches them the monthly is optional, and costs you the months you should've been paid for. |
