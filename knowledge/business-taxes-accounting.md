- Rule: "ordinary and necessary," keep receipts + clean records.

## Entity structure — the "$15k on paper" correction
- **Sole prop / single-member LLC (default):** pass-through — **ALL net profit is taxed to Bryson personally**, whether withdrawn or left in the account. No W-2 salary from yourself; paying yourself less changes nothing. So "only make $15k on paper" does NOT work here (and under-reporting = illegal evasion).
- **S-corp (LLC electing S-corp):** the REAL version — pay a **reasonable salary** (W-2) + take the rest as **distributions that skip the 15.3% self-employment tax.** Catches: salary must be reasonable (IRS reclassifies fake-low pay); adds payroll + separate return + CPA (~$1–2k/yr); only worth it once net profit is **~$40–50k+**. Premature at his current stage.

## What to actually do (legal tax reduction)
1. **Form an LLC** (AZ ~$50) — liability protection; taxed as sole prop by default (simple).
2. **Separate business bank account** — run everything through it.
3. **Track income/expenses/mileage** — QuickBooks Self-Employed, Wave (free), or a spreadsheet.
4. **Retirement accounts** — **Solo 401(k) or SEP-IRA** deduct a big chunk of profit into his own retirement (legal deferral + wealth-building).
5. **Set aside ~25–30% for taxes** and pay **quarterly estimated taxes** (self-employed owe 4×/yr; skipping = penalties).
6. **Revisit S-corp** with a CPA once profit is consistently ~$40–50k+.

## WHO TO HIRE (the answer Bryson wants when he revisits this)
- **Primary: a small-business / self-employed CPA.** Handles entity structure, catches every deduction, sets up quarterly estimates, times the S-corp, files the return. A one-time consult (~$200–400) or a light retainer is one of the highest-ROI dollars going self-employed. Look for: works with solo founders / self-employed, does **proactive planning** (not just April filing), AZ-based or remote-friendly.
- **Cheaper alternative: an Enrolled Agent (EA)** — federally licensed for tax prep + planning, usually less than a CPA; fine for a simple early-stage business.
- **Optional: a bookkeeper** for ongoing transaction categorization (or DIY with the tools above until it's worth outsourcing).
- **Referral path:** the **AZ business attorney** he's already going to use for the contract can usually refer a good small-business CPA.
- **Payroll service (e.g., Gusto):** only once he's an S-corp paying himself a W-2 salary.
- **Not needed early:** a tax *attorney* (only for complex/dispute situations).
