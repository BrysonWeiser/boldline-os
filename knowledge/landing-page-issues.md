**➕ NO ITEM LEFT ALONE ON A ROW (2026-09-01, Bryson: "remember make sure everything is uniform and looks good", with a screenshot).**
- **The defect he saw:** four benefits, three across one row, the fourth hanging by itself under the left edge beside a wide gap. It reads like the page broke.
- **Cause:** `.bene` / `.benum` were pinned to `grid-template-columns:repeat(3,1fr)` above 720px no matter how many items the copy writer produced, and the generator prompt asks for **"3-4 benefits"**. So a stranded fourth was the COMMON case, not an edge case, and it would have shipped on most client pages.
- **Fix (landing.mjs):** `gridFor(n)` puts a count class on the wrapper (`g1..g5`) and the CSS gives each count a column count that divides cleanly. 2 -> 2 across. 3 -> 3. **4 -> two-by-two**, opening to four across at >=1100px for CARDS only. 5 -> 3 then 2 (a full row plus a pair, not a row plus an orphan). 6+ -> 3 across. The `list` style stays a single column by design at every width; a stack is not an orphan.
- 🔴 **GOTCHA — the first version of this fix was completely inert and looked correct.** The four-across rule was written ABOVE the two-across rule. Same specificity (`.bene.g4` = 0,2,0), so **source order decides**, and the later two-across rule silently won. Reading the source would never have caught it. Anything added to this stylesheet at equal specificity must go AFTER the rule it needs to beat, and the media queries are kept in ascending order for that reason.
- **Numbered benefits deliberately stay two-by-two even on a wide screen** — they lay out sideways (number badge beside the text) and go cramped in a quarter-width column.
- **Enforced, not remembered:** `tests/verify-landing-grid.mjs` renders the REAL page, pulls the REAL stylesheet out of it, **resolves the cascade the way a browser does** (last matching rule wins, media queries evaluated at 390/768/1280/1600) and asserts no count from 1 to 6 in any of the three styles ever leaves one item alone on a final row. It includes the source-order mutation, so the inert version fails. 199 checks.
- Also re-confirmed with a headless probe measuring real row membership by top edge across 3 styles x 4 counts x 4 widths, and by eye at 1600px.
- **A stacked single-column list is NOT an orphan** — a first version of the probe flagged all 24 of them. Only a grid that is genuinely multi-column can strand an item.
- Fixed a stale assertion in `tests/verify-market-research.mjs` while here: it pinned the trust row to a globe emoji that the no-emoji rule had since removed, so it was failing on wording that had not changed. It now checks the behaviour (falls back to the national reach line) plus that the row carries no emoji at all.

- 🔴 **THE BUG THE BROWSER AUDIT CAUGHT, AND THE REASON IT EXISTS: `*` DOES NOT MATCH PSEUDO-ELEMENTS.** The reduced-motion block was `*{animation:none!important}`, which has been in this file for months and looked airtight. **➕ THE REVEAL KEEPS HAPPENING, AND THE HOVER LIFTS ACTUALLY LIFT (2026-09-02, Bryson: "make sure the micro animations such as a scroll and clicking, giving, etc. always happen (this is mainly for scrolling to make sure the up and down animation happens even after a person has scrolled through the whole page)").**
- 🔴 **HE WAS RIGHT AND IT WAS WORSE THAN HE THOUGHT: MOST OF THE PAGE NEVER ANIMATED AT ALL.** Two things combined. The observer called `io.unobserve` the first time an element appeared, so nothing could ever play twice. And the 1.5s safety net revealed EVERY element on the page whether it had been reached or not. So on a real visit, everything below the first screenful was already marked revealed before the visitor got near it and simply sat there. The scroll reveal had effectively only ever worked for the hero.
- **The net cannot just be deleted** — it is the only thing standing between us and the original blank-sections bug in a context nobody scrolls (preview iframe, screenshot, a visitor who never moves). **So the fix is four pieces and each one covers one of the four ways this breaks:** (1) a REVEAL observer with a tight margin that adds `in` and never un-observes; (2) a RE-ARM observer with a `160px` margin that removes `in` once an element is clear of the screen, its region strictly containing the reveal observer's so the two can never fight at the boundary; (3) the 1.5s SAFETY NET, unchanged in what it guarantees; (4) FIRST SCROLL cancels the net and re-arms anything already off screen. **Without (4) there are two broken visitors**: one who scrolls before 1.5s (the net fires anyway and reveals everything below) and the slow reader who scrolls after it (the net has already run and the re-arm observer will never fire again, because those elements' intersection state never changed). Both get a dead page.
- 🔴 **SECOND BUG FOUND THE SAME WAY: EVERY HOVER LIFT ON THE PAGE WAS DEAD ON ARRIVAL.** Cards, chips, steps and review tiles are all `.reveal`, and `.js .reveal.in` (0,3,0) outranks `.bcard:hover` (0,2,0), so it pinned `transform:none` and nothing ever moved. The box-shadow and border-colour parts of each hover rule worked, which is why it looked plausible. **Fix: the reveal moved to the `translate` property**, which the browser composes with `transform` instead of competing with it. The reveal owns `translate`, every hover and press owns `transform`. Side benefit: `--rv` is now a bare Y distance fed into `translate:0 var(--rv)`, so a sideways reveal is **structurally impossible** rather than merely absent.
- **Verified by driving the page, because neither bug is visible in the source.** Both read as correct CSS/JS. `tools/audit-landing-motion.js` gained a REPLAY check (scroll to a section, away, back, it must animate each time, on both the early-scroller and slow-reader paths) and a HOVER check (hover must actually change the computed transform on `.bcard/.step/.rev/.chip`, plus the press on the main button, which is all a phone visitor gets). Its main sweep now READS DOWN the page a screen at a time instead of measuring once from the top, because an element far below the fold is now supposed to be armed and hidden. `AUDIT_ONLY=behaviour` skips the 144-load sweep when bisecting.
- 🔴 **A MUTATION ESCAPED, AGAIN FOR A TIMING REASON.** Removing `clearTimeout(net)` caused no failure, because the replay check scrolled to the section at ~1s and reached it BEFORE the net fired at 1.5s, so it passed for the wrong reason. The check now sits near the top until the net has definitely had its chance, THEN asks whether the far-below section was revealed behind the visitor's back. All four mutations caught after that.
- Static side: `verify-landing-motion.mjs` at 154 checks, now also asserting no `unobserve`, exactly two observers, the re-arm margin, the net, `clearTimeout`, the rearm, a passive once-only scroll handler, and 🔴 that the reveal never takes `transform` back.
- 🔴 **FIFTH TIME: a backtick inside the `css` template literal kills the whole renderer.** Also stripped the opening delimiter by accident while cleaning them out. The suite catches it (`verify-functions-resolve`), a one-off probe does not, so run the suite before believing a probe.
