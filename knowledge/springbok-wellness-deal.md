## 🔴 His one real objection: what counts as a qualified lead

He has been burned twice before, and both are real failure modes rather than nerves:

1. **People book an appointment and never become a patient.** A booking is not a customer. If the ads optimise toward bookings, Google will faithfully buy more people who book and never turn up, which is the exact trap the conversion loop exists to avoid (KB `conversion-loop`).
2. **Sales enquiries through the ads.** People using the form to sell him something, not to become a patient.

**What Bryson told him**, which is accurate: the ads learn from what BoldLine reports back as qualified, and the two of them get on a Google Meet at the end of each month before he is invoiced to agree which leads counted. Same arrangement as Sebastian.

### Three things to get right, recorded before they bite

🔴 **The learning phase is slow, and month one will be noisy.** Google wants roughly 15 to 30 conversions in 30 days per campaign before its bidding is any good. A single clinic on a starting budget may not hit that for weeks. "The learning phase will filter them out" is TRUE but not FAST, and promising it as a month-one outcome sets him up to be disappointed exactly when he is deciding whether this works. Say plainly that the first month is the one that teaches the system.

🔴 **Sales enquiries are a day-one fix, not a learning-phase fix.** They are blocked by negative keywords and by the junk flagger on the billing review, not by bidding. Do not let those two get bundled together in his head, because the fix and the timeline are different.

🔴 **AGREE THE DEFINITION IN WRITING AFTER THE FIRST CALL, then apply it.** A per-lead deal where the client decides what counted, monthly, after seeing the bill, is a deal that gets renegotiated every month and drifts one way only. The monthly call should settle **edge cases against a written standard**, not re-decide the standard. Write it down after month one and hold it.

## The ads: what he asked for, and the recommendation

He wants **TMJ** and symptom searches like **lower back pain** and **neck pain**.

**TMJ** is the temporomandibular joint, the jaw hinge. Symptoms are jaw pain, clicking, headaches, often from grinding. Chiropractors do treat it. It is a genuinely good niche: low competition, motivated searchers. But **low volume**, so it cannot carry a campaign on its own.

**Bryson's instinct to open as "a chiropractor" rather than one service is right.** The refinement is which searches to buy first, because they are not equal:

| Search | What it means | Buy it? |
|---|---|---|
| `chiropractor near me`, `chiropractor <town>` | already sold on the category, shopping for a provider | 🟢 **First.** Dearest clicks, best conversion, this is the money |
| `back pain chiropractor`, `sciatica chiropractor` | has the problem AND has decided on the solution | 🟢 **First.** Same intent, cheaper |
| `TMJ treatment`, `TMJ specialist` | named condition, actively seeking care | 🟡 **Its own ad group**, once the core is running |
| `lower back pain`, `why does my neck hurt` | has a symptom, has NOT decided on a chiropractor | 🔴 **Hold.** Huge volume, cheap clicks, mostly people wanting a free stretch video. This is where a small budget dies |

So: open with the provider-intent terms, add TMJ as its own group early because it is cheap and motivated, and only go after bare symptom searches once there is conversion data for Google to lean on. The generator already builds intent-themed ad groups, so this falls out of how the OS works if the groups are ticked deliberately.
