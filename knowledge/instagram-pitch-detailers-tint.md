## The plan given
- **Two jobs, two tools.** Posts and reels build trust (they will look at his profile before replying). DMs to
  businesses are what actually start conversations. 20 to 30 personal DMs a day, not mass copy-paste (Instagram limits
  and flags that).
- **Pick the plan from their profile, before the DM:**
  - Link in bio is a Linktree, Facebook page or nothing -> lead with the WEBSITE (offer a free mockup).
  - Good website, but the feed shows gaps or "booking now!" posts -> lead with ADS (pay per qualified lead).
  - Established (multiple vans or a shop, lots of reviews, years in) with a weak or no site -> COMPLETE. More often a
    tint shop (physical location, Google profile matters) than a one-person mobile detailer, who usually cannot carry
    $500 a month plus an ad budget yet: start them on the website or ads and upgrade later.
- **DM shape:** specific compliment on their work, one honest observation, one free low-effort offer, a question that is
  easy to say yes to. The hook is a free mockup of their website, made with the OS "Build their demo website"
  (KB demo-sites). Never send a mockup unasked in the first message; ask first.
- **Content ideas (his house style: face + captions, KB video-editing-style):** "Why your Instagram followers don't
  book", "Your link in bio is losing you tint jobs", a before/after of a detailer site (use the marketing site's
  /examples/ or /industries/ pages, never a real business without permission), "What happens after someone clicks
  your ad", "You only pay for real leads".
- **Profile:** bio says who it is for and the promise ("I get detailers and tint shops booked. Pay per real lead.");
  link straight to boldlinemedia.com/industries/car-detailing/ or /window-tint/; highlights for Websites, How it
  works, Pricing.
- Honesty: ads = pay per qualified lead, no monthly fee, ad budget paid to Google/Meta on their own card. Complete =
  $500/mo first year then $375, plus the lead fee. No em dashes or emojis in anything a prospect reads. Never
  "local businesses".

## Follow-up 2026-11-10: a website that exists but is weak or only one page
- Lead with something they can check themselves, never "your site is bad": one page = Google has one page to show for
  every search (ceramic coating, paint correction, home tint all compete for it); the booking button is below the
  fold on a phone; no reviews on the site; an old year in the footer; no padlock. Lead Scout's free website check
  lists exactly these (KB website-prospects).
- Two offers that are easy to say yes to: the free Lead-Leak Check (boldlinemedia.com/free-check/) and a free mockup
  (OS demo site). Frame it as an upgrade that keeps their domain, photos and reviews, not a replacement.
- Plan: established shop -> Complete (site built free). Solo detailer who mainly wants more jobs -> ads, because every
  ad plan comes with its own landing page, so the weak site is not in the way; offer the website later.

## Worked example 2026-11-10: a brand-new solo mobile detailer (Phoenix/Avondale, bookable.bio link page)
Profile: 3 posts, 21 followers, Meta Verified, owner on the account, link-in-bio page (bookable.bio) with services and
prices ($200 first full detail, $80 recurring maintenance), Text Us / Call Us, gallery, hours. Verdict given: his link
page already does a website's job (services, prices, booking by text), so DON'T lead with a website; his real gap is
being found (21 followers, likely not on Google Maps yet). Probably not a buyer today: a one-person shop just starting
usually cannot carry $500/mo of ad budget plus lead fees. Play: value first, no pitch in message one (compliment real
work, ask where customers come from), give a free useful tip (set up a Google Business Profile), meet in person since
he is in Phoenix, and come back with ads once he is doing steady jobs and wants more. The recurring $80 maintenance is
the money angle later: one ad lead that becomes a regular is worth far more than one $200 detail.
- Same example, owner's personal account: college track athlete (national champion, All-American), 1,073 followers vs
  the business's 21. Advice: DM the owner's PERSONAL account (where he actually is), open on his real achievement then
  the business, and make the first free tip "post your detailing work as a Collab with your personal account" (his own
  audience is 50x the shop's), plus the Google profile tip. Likely a student or recent grad running it on the side,
  which confirms: relationship now, ads later. General rule: with a one-person shop, find and DM the owner's personal
  account.
- His reply (2026-11-10): clients from word of mouth, Facebook, TikTok, Instagram; a base of customers from his old
  mobile mechanic work; cards and flyers; already shared the detailing page on his personal story. Next move given:
  point out Google is the one channel missing (people searching are ready to book that day), offer the GBP steps (KB
  gbp-setup-howto), suggest texting his old mechanic customers a detailing offer, and ask the two qualifying questions
  (details per week now, how many he could handle). His answers decide when ads make sense.
